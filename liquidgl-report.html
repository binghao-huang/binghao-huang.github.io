liquidGL — What It Can Do (with live examples)

Each effect below has a live, interactive demo — drag the sliders and the real liquidGL effect updates in real time over a photo background.

1. Real-time refraction / lensing maybe

Snapshots the page behind the element and bends it through a lens shader — the background warps like real curved glass (CSS only blurs, it can't do this). Drag refraction up and watch the photo distort through the panel.

refraction demo

2. Bevel & edge depth good fit

Simulates the thick, light-bending rim of a glass slab — the strongest "iOS glass" cue. bevelDepth = how pronounced the edge lens is; bevelWidth = how far in the bevel reaches. Drag both.

bevel demo

3. Frost (blur) good fit

A frosted/privacy-glass blur on top of the refraction. 0 = crystal clear, higher = more diffuse. This is closest to your current CSS glass — but here it's combined with real refraction.

frost demo

4. Specular highlights good fit

Animated glints of light that drift across the surface over time — the "alive" sheen of Apple's Liquid Glass. Toggle it on/off and watch the moving highlight.

specular demo
(look for the slow-moving bright streak)

5. Dynamic shadow good fit

A soft drop-shadow rendered beneath the pane to lift it off the page. Toggle to see the panel detach from / sink into the background.

shadow demo

6. Magnification maybe

Scales the lensed content like a magnifying glass. >1 enlarges the background through the glass, <1 shrinks it (fish-eye). Drag to zoom the photo through the panel.

magnify demo

7. 3D tilt on hover maybe

The pane tilts in 3D following your cursor, like tipping a glass card. Turn it on, then move your mouse over the panel. tiltFactor sets the depth in degrees.

Minimal usage

<!-- deps + library -->
<script src="html2canvas.min.js"></script>
<script src="liquidGL.js"></script>

<nav class="liquidGL"> ... </nav>

const glass = liquidGL({
  target: '.liquidGL',
  refraction: 0.05, bevelDepth: 0.1, frost: 2,
  specular: true, magnify: 1.05, tilt: true
});

11. Caveats & performance

  • Needs html2canvas (~150KB) + a full-page snapshot at load.
  • Cross-origin content (YouTube/Bilibili iframes) can't be snapshotted — can stall the reveal and leave glass hidden unless a fallback is added. (The bug we hit before.)
  • Safari can be unstable when a glass element exceeds ~50% of the viewport.
  • Tested up to ~30 instances; all must share the same z-index (one shared canvas).
  • No-WebGL devices fall back to CSS backdrop-filter (cruder than your tuned CSS glass).
  • Cannot refract pure CSS animations; no chromatic-aberration / color-tint / destroy() in the documented API.

12. My take for your site

Best candidate try

One floating glass nav: refraction + magnify + specular + tilt, with your publication videos refracting behind it as you scroll. Single element = low risk.

Situational maybe

Subtle tilt on a hero element, or an Edge/Frost preset on a few featured cards. Test on one first.

Avoid skip

Glass on all ~50 cards (perf + cross-origin snapshot bug) and scroll-sync (no smooth-scroll lib). Current CSS glass is lighter.
